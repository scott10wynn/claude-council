# Commodities Seasonality Trading

Commodity seasonality is anchored in **physics and biology** — crops grow on fixed schedules, refineries need maintenance, festivals recur on calendars. This makes it more mechanically reliable than equity seasonality (which is largely behavioral), but it is still a guide, not a guarantee. Always use technical confirmation before entry.

## 1. Gold (XAU/USD)

### Monthly Performance (50-year averages)

| Month | Avg Return | Win Rate | Driver |
|-------|-----------|----------|--------|
| Jan | +1.8–1.9% | 70–80% | "January Effect" — strongest month |
| Feb | Positive | High | Chinese New Year tail-end demand |
| Mar | −0.6% | Weak | Post-CNY lull; year's weakest month |
| Jun | −0.4% | Weak | Second weakest |
| Jul | Turn positive | Improving | Seasonal low often forms July 1–10 |
| Aug | +1.5–2.0% | Strong | Indian pre-festival buying begins |
| **Sep** | **+2.1%** | **Strong** | **Strongest average month** |
| Oct–Nov | +1.4% | Strong | Diwali + Indian wedding season |
| Dec | +1.5% | Strong | Pre-CNY demand + portfolio rebalancing |

### Primary Seasonal Window
**July 6 – February 21** (following year): average gains of 6.96–11.27% over 50 years, more than double the overall annual average of 5.18%.

### Demand Drivers
1. **Chinese New Year (Jan/Feb):** Gift jewelry and gold coin purchases surge Dec–Jan
2. **Indian Diwali/Dhanteras (Oct–Nov):** ~160 tonnes purchased; 15 of 24 years saw 6–31% gains post-Diwali
3. **Indian wedding season (Sep–Dec):** Sustained buying
4. **January Effect:** Year-end tax-loss selling creates Dec dip → reinvestment flows in Jan
5. **Institutional year-end rebalancing:** Flows into gold

### Entry/Exit Strategy
- **Primary entry:** Early July (seasonal low) or mid-December
- **Primary exit:** Mid-February (post-CNY peak) or mid-April
- **Secondary trade:** Long in August for Sep–Nov Indian festival season rally

## 2. Crude Oil (WTI/Brent)

### Seasonal Cycle

| Season | Months | Behavior | Driver |
|--------|--------|----------|--------|
| Winter | Dec–Feb | Lowest of year | Post-holiday demand lull |
| Spring Rally | Feb–May | Gradually rises | Refinery restarts, driving season build |
| Summer | Jun–Aug | Near top of annual range | Peak driving demand, RBOB specs |
| Fall Weakness | Sep–Oct | **Historically weakest** | Refinery maintenance, demand lull |
| Winter Rebound | Nov–Dec | Heating demand onset | |

### Key Drivers
**Driving Season:** US gasoline demand peaks Memorial Day through Labor Day. Refineries switch to summer-grade RBOB starting March 1 (more expensive to produce, widens crack spreads). WTI typically bottoms in February and rallies through May.

**Refinery Maintenance:**
- Spring turnaround: Jan–Mar (before peak driving season)
- Fall turnaround: Sep–Oct (after driving season, before heating season)
Both create short-term supply/demand dislocations.

### Win Rate Data
- February–June bullish window: ~64% win rate (profitable in ~14 of 22 years)
- June (Brent): +4% average, **78% win rate** — strongest risk-adjusted month
- September–October: historically weakest

### Entry/Exit Strategy
- **Entry:** Late January / early February (pre-refinery restart seasonal low)
- **Exit:** Late May / early June (peak driving season complete)
- **Avoid / short:** September–October (refinery maintenance, post-driving lull)

## 3. Agricultural Commodities

### Corn

| Period | Behavior | Driver |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Feb–Mar | Rising | "Buy acres" — pre-planting uncertainty |
| Apr–May | Volatile/higher | Planting progress uncertainty |
| Jun–Jul | Peak uncertainty | Weather market (drought risk) |
| **Sep–Oct** | **Seasonal low** | Harvest pressure, farmer selling |
| Nov–Jan | Recovery | Storage premium builds, export demand |

**Strategy:** Buy November/December after harvest lows; exit March–April when spring planting premium is priced in.

**Pattern reliability:** Corn bottoms at fall harvest in roughly 9 of 10 years.

### Soybeans

| Period | Behavior | Driver |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Apr–Jun | Rising | Planting uncertainty, South American crop clarity |
| Jul | Often rally peak | Last chance before crop visibility |
| Aug–Sep | Weakening | Crop size becomes clear |
| **Oct–Nov** | **Seasonal low** | Peak harvest, farmer selling |
| Dec–Feb | Recovery | Export season, crush demand |

**Strategy:** Buy early July (pre-harvest rally); exit late August (before harvest pressure). Alternative: buy late October/November for winter recovery.

### Wheat (Winter Wheat — US HRW/SRW)

| Period | Behavior | Driver |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Apr–Jun | Rising | Crop condition fears |
| **July** | **Seasonal low** | Winter wheat harvest (Jun–Jul) |
| Aug–Sep | Recovery | New crop uncertainty, export demand |

**Strategy:** Buy August (post-harvest low); exit May–June (pre-harvest premium).

### Agricultural Summary

| Commodity | Typical Low | Typical High | Entry | Exit |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|-------|------|
| Corn | Sep–Oct | Mar–Apr | Oct–Nov | Feb–Mar |
| Soybeans | Oct–Nov | Jun–Jul | Nov–Dec | Jul–Aug |
| Winter Wheat | July | May–Jun | August | May |

## 5. Risk Management for Seasonal Commodity Trades

### Position Sizing
- Never risk more than **1–2% of account equity** per seasonal trade
- Cap total open risk across all commodity positions at **5–6% of account equity**
- Formula: Position Size = (Account × Risk %) / ((Entry − Stop) × Contract Size)

### Stop-Loss Placement
- Stop at a **logical invalidation point**, not arbitrary dollar amounts
- Gold: below prior major swing low that would negate the seasonal thesis
- Crude: below the February seasonal low (if long for spring rally)
- Grains: outside the range of the previous harvest low/high

### Seasonal Spread Trades (Lower Risk Alternative)
Trade **calendar spreads** (long nearby / short deferred) instead of outright futures to isolate the seasonal component with reduced outright market risk and lower margin requirements.

Example: Long March corn / Short December corn to express the "buy acres" premium.

### Futures Roll Management
- Be aware of **contango** (futures pricier than spot) — erodes long positions on roll
- **Backwardation** (futures cheaper than spot) — benefits longs
- Roll into the contract with highest open interest; avoid holding through delivery month

### Factors That Override Seasonality
1. Macro shocks (war, currency crises, OPEC+ decisions for oil)
2. Abnormal weather (La Nina/El Nino for ags)
3. Demand destruction (recessions kill oil/commodity seasonality)
4. Policy changes (export bans, SPR releases)

**Rule:** Seasonality improves timing when used with technical confirmation. It provides context, not instruction — never subordinate risk management to calendar expectations.

## Key Difference: Commodity vs. Equity Seasonality

| Feature | Commodities | Equities |
|---------|------------|---------|
| Root cause | Physical supply/demand cycles (harvest, weather, refinery schedules) | Behavioral, institutional (tax loss, fund flows) |
| Predictability | Anchored in repeatable natural/industrial cycles | Psychological — less reliable |
| "Sell in May" | Does NOT apply — crude and gold often positive in May | Applies most directly to broad equity indices |
| Volatility | Very high (oil 6–14% monthly std dev) | Lower |
| Leverage | Futures = high implicit leverage; size carefully | Cash equities; leverage is opt-in |
